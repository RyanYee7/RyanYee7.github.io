Where Modern Surfing Began

Duke Kahanamoku was a Hawaiian swimmer, Olympic gold medalist, and one of the most important figures in the history of surfing. He helped introduce surfing to the world and became known as the father of modern surfing.

Capturing Kelly Slater Greatest Surfs

Kelly Slater is one of the most successful competitive surfers of all time, known for his powerful style and long career. He has won many world titles and helped push surfing into the modern professional era.

How Gerry Lopez Became a Symbol of Hawaiian Surfing

Gerry Lopez is a legendary surfer known for his smooth, calm style at Pipeline in Hawaii. His graceful approach to dangerous waves earned him the nickname “Mr. Pipeline.”

Inside Surfing’s Most Dangerous Moment

Three-time ASP World Champion Andy Irons grew up on the shallow, hazardous reefs of Kauai and became globally renowned for conquering the world's most dangerous surf.

Do You Have What It Takes to Ride Giants?

German surfer Sebastian Steudtner holds the Guinness World Record for the largest wave ever surfed, riding a massive 86-foot wave at Praia do Norte in Nazaré, Portugal.

Banzai Pipeline

On the North Shore of Oʻahu, Hawaii, Banzai Pipeline is one of surfing’s most legendary and dangerous waves. Breaking over a shallow reef, its perfectly formed barrels, raw power, and high-stakes atmosphere have made it a proving ground for generations of surfers.

Jeffrey’s Bay

Along the Eastern Cape of South Africa, Jeffrey’s Bay—often called J-Bay—is revered for its long, fast, flawless right-hand point break. With sections that race down the line for hundreds of meters, it offers a rare blend of speed, shape, and rhythm.

Teahupo'o

Located on the southwest coast of Tahiti in French Polynesia, Teahupo’o is famous for thick, glassy barrels that break with extraordinary force over a shallow coral reef. The wave is both mesmerizing and intimidating, often described as one of the heaviest and most visually dramatic breaks in the world.

Uluwatu

Beneath the limestone cliffs of Bali’s Bukit Peninsula in Indonesia, Uluwatu offers a series of beautiful left-hand reef breaks with remarkable consistency. Its tropical setting, rich surf culture, and long, clean waves have made it one of Indonesia’s most iconic surf destinations.

Nazaré

On the Atlantic coast of Nazaré, Portugal, this legendary big-wave spot has become synonymous with some of the largest surfable waves on Earth. A deep underwater canyon funnels massive swells toward Praia do Norte, creating a dramatic spectacle beneath the town’s cliffs and lighthouse.
